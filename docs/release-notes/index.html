    Upload and match a month's payroll

    Put the month's payroll in one zip with four folders: timesheets, payroll registers, pay stubs and bank statements. A sample zip shows the layout. The files are read in the background, so you can close the window and come back.

    Auto-Match then finds each payroll item's timesheet, register and evidence of payment by the person's name, pay and pay period inside the files. Anything it can't match, you match by hand. Nothing is saved until you press Save.

    Good to know

    • Staff payments whose bank line wasn't found are listed after matching. Find the line or draw a box around it, now or later, one row at a time from the month page.

    Match receipts

    On a month's page, "Upload receipts" takes many PDFs or photos at once. Each is read for its amount, date and vendor, and when exactly one expense of the month has the same amount within three days and no invoice or receipt yet, it is matched to that expense automatically, as if you had attached it.

    The new "Match receipts" panel lists the receipts still unmatched, each with its best match and why it fits (the amount, the days apart, the vendor). Choose "Match" to confirm it or pick another expense, "unmatch" to undo one, and "not for this grant" to set a receipt aside; you can bring it back.

    Good to know
    • A PDF with its own text is read from that text. A photo or a scanned PDF is read by AI only when you tick "Read the amount, date and vendor with AI" in the upload dialog (it starts unticked every time); otherwise you type its amount, date and vendor. The upload's line in the access log says when AI read it.
    • Whatever was read, "edit" lets you correct it; the suggestions follow what you type.
    • Personal documents (pay stubs, eligibility records) are never read and never shown in the panel.
    • Every upload, match, unmatch and setting aside is in the access log, automatic matches marked as such. Once the month-end closing is approved, the receipts are locked with the rest of the month.

    Import transactions

    Typing every expense by hand is slow when your accounting system already has them. Export the transactions (in QuickBooks: a Transaction Report, exported to CSV or Excel), then choose "Import transactions" on the engagement's ledger step or on a month's page.

    The import matches the file's columns to the fields of the expense form; you check each one. Next, choose the budget line each category of the file is charged to, all at once, or skip its rows. The preview shows every row: the month it goes to, or why it stays out. Each imported row becomes an expense line in the month of its date, across several months if the file spans them, and goes through the same checks as one you type.

    Good to know
    • Save the mapping by name (say "QuickBooks mapping") and the next file with the same columns uses it straight away. Owners and bookkeepers import and save mappings; auditors don't.
    • A row imported before is flagged and never added twice. Rows outside the contract period, in a month whose closing is approved or that is submitted, or with a negative amount (a refund) are listed as not imported, with the reason.
    • A month that does not exist yet is started as a draft. Each import is one line in the access log, with the counts and totals per month.
    • The file is read and then let go: it is not stored, and no row of it is ever sent to AI. The columns are guessed from their names by rules. Only when you press "Suggest the mapping with AI" are the column names (never the rows) sent to AI, and the import's line in the access log then says the columns were suggested with AI.
    • This is a file you export, not a connection to QuickBooks: nothing changes in your accounting system.
    • No accounting system? Download the sample file, fill it in and import it.

    Email in your receipts

    Each workspace has one receipts address that does not change. An owner picks it once under Settings. Owners and bookkeepers email a receipt there from their own address, with the engagement's code in the subject (set it in the engagement's Details). Each PDF, PNG or JPG attachment is filed into the month of the date on the receipt, or the day the email arrived, and matched to its expense when there is one clear match, just as if you had uploaded it yourself.

    The receipts inbox on the workspace page lists every email your owners and bookkeepers sent and what became of it. One that could not be placed (no code and more than one active engagement, or a closed month) waits there for you: choose the engagement and month and file it. One that failed can be run again.

    Good to know
    • Only owners and bookkeepers of the workspace can send in receipts, from the address they sign in with. Email from anyone else is refused and not listed, and so is email whose sender cannot be verified.
    • With no code in the subject, a receipt goes to the workspace's only active engagement.
    • Up to 10 attachments of 10 MB each per email; anything else is listed as skipped. Pictures in the body of an email, such as a logo in a signature, are not filed.
    • An emailed receipt is read from its PDF's text. It is read by AI only when an owner turns on "Read emailed receipts with AI" on the receipts address card; it starts off.
    • Nobody gets an email back, and no document is ever sent out by email.

    See whether the app is up

    The footer of every page, signed in or not, now ends with a small status badge. It says "All services are online" when everything is working, and changes when something is not. Click it to open the public status page.

    Behind it, the app is checked every few minutes for two things: an upload of proof that failed through no fault of the person, and any request the app could not answer. If either happened in the last 15 minutes, the team hears about it by email, often before anyone reports it.

    Good to know
    • The status page shows only whether each part is working. It never shows a workspace, a record or why something failed.

    Request edits on a month

    The month's bundle

    A month's page has a new button, "Download the month's bundle". It gives you one zip to send with the invoice. Inside is Bundle.pdf, one PDF in this order: the signed monthly report (or a cover page saying it is not on file yet), a ledger summary with every line and the totals by report line, each line's supporting records in ledger order, then the payroll proof, with each marked payroll line highlighted on its bank statement. Each record starts with a short page naming the item it supports.

    Spreadsheets and Word files can't go into a PDF, so they sit beside it in a Supporting files folder, named by item number. Index.csv lists every record, the item it supports and the pages it is on. A README says the month's status, who approved the month-end closing and when, and whether it has been submitted.

    Good to know
    • Only people in the workspace can download it, from the month's page. There is no link to share.
    • Personal records (pay stubs, eligibility records) are never in the bundle, whoever downloads it: the funder did not ask for them. The README says how many were left out.
    • It works for a draft month too, so you can check it before the closing is approved. Every download is in the access log.
    • One bundle holds up to 100 MB of records. For a bigger month, download the audit package from the engagement page.

    Tags

    Some participants count toward one funding stream and some toward another; some are under 18. Tags let you say so on the record. Owners set up tag types and their values in the workspace settings ("Tags"), and choose what each type applies to: engagements, expenses, payroll items, participants, or any of them.

    A new workspace starts with one type, "Funding source", and you edit it like any other: add a source, rename one, or remove it. A new engagement picks its sources of funds in the setup steps (and later under Details), and the monthly report form prints them under Source of funds. Tag an expense when you add or edit it, a payroll item with "tags" on its row, and a participant when you add them or with "tags" in the participants list. Every tag is chosen from a dropdown you can search and tick several in. The month's ledger and the participants list each have a filter to show only one tag.

    Good to know
    • Only values from the list: no free-text tags. Bookkeepers can suggest a new one under "More lists"; auditors read tags and change none.
    • A participant's tags are personal, like the rest of their file. The access log says their tags changed, never which ones. Payroll items are a participant's pay and are logged the same way; an expense's tags are logged by name.
    • Renaming a value renames it on every record. Removing a value, or a whole type, takes it off every engagement, expense, payroll item and participant that has it; before you save, you see how many. A closed or submitted month does not change: while one of its records has the tag, the removal is refused until you reopen that month.

    Budget lines your way

    Step 4 showed the approved budget but gave you no way to enter it. Now it does. Open the engagement, find "Approved budget" in step 4, and choose "+ Add line" or "Change" on a line to set its amount and narrative, or remove it.

    The lines come from your workspace's budget items, a list owners keep in the workspace settings beside the sources of funds. Each item rolls into one line of the monthly report. When you set up an engagement, the contract step lists the items, all ticked; untick the ones this budget does not use.

    Good to know
    • Owners and bookkeepers change the budget; auditors read it. Every line added, changed or removed is in the access log with its amount.
    • Every change to the budget re-checks every month, and puts every closed and submitted month back to draft, because they were closed against the old budget. The line you are changing tells you first how many; after saving, a notice names them. A closed month is sent back with the reason on its page; a submitted one is reopened, with its earlier submission, approval and signed copy kept in its history. Approve their closing again and send them again. Drafts are not changed.
    • Attaching or detaching a record in a participant's file does the same, but only for the closed and submitted months whose checks it changed.
    • Renaming an item renames it on every engagement. To move an item to another report line, remove it and add a new one. Bookkeepers can suggest an item under "More lists".
    • Every workspace starts with the usual categories (Personnel, Fringe Benefits, Travel and the rest), so existing budgets are unchanged.

    Month-end closing

    We had this backwards. The approval a month needs is your company's own: your CEO, or whoever approves for you, reviews the month before it goes to the funder, often offline. That is the month-end closing, and it replaces "Record the funder's decision".

    On the month's page, once nothing is blocking, choose "Approve month-end closing" and record who approved it (your engagement's authorizer is filled in), the day they approved it, and a note if you want one. The ledger is then locked. Next, sign the funder's form, attach the signed copy, and submit by the due day. You can only submit a month whose closing is approved.

    Good to know
    • Something to fix after the approval? "Send back" with the reason makes the month a draft again; the reason shows on the month until it is approved again.
    • A typo in a month already submitted? "Reopen to correct", with the reason, makes it a draft again. Its documents stay. Correct it, approve its closing again, attach the corrected signed copy beside the old one and submit it again. Nothing of the earlier submission is lost: "Earlier submissions" on the month lists each one, with the day it was submitted, who approved its closing and when, who reopened it, when and why, and the signed copy as it went to the funder. That copy can no longer be replaced or removed. The month's bundle and the audit package hold the earlier signed copies and a Reopenings.csv. The reopening is in the access log.
    • The app no longer records the funder's answer (approved or denied for payment, invoice number). The printed form leaves the funder's review section blank for them.
    • Months already submitted stay submitted. A month the funder had denied is a draft again, with the funder's reason shown as why it was sent back.
    • The engagement's month checklist and step 7 now read: ledger complete, month-end closing approved, signed report on file, submitted by the due day.

    Some teams only want the monthly financials: the budget, the months and their proof. When you set up an engagement, the first step now asks what you want to track for this grant or contract: the proposal, the award letter and the signed contract are each a checkbox, and the financials are always kept.

    The setup asks only for what you tick. Leaving out the signed contract keeps step 4 for its dates and budget lines and drops only the file.

    Good to know

    • The audit package's README lists the records not kept here.
    • The amount and the contract dates are no longer required at setup; the engagement page asks for them until they are in.

    What the app does today

    Audit Differently has one job: show how a team spent grant or contract money, and whether the proof is complete, before the funder's auditor asks. It is built for the bookkeeper who accounts for that money. This first note is the baseline, everything the app does today in one place; later notes will each cover one change. Nothing is deployed yet: it all runs in the local demo on fictional data, as the workspace Riverbend Youth Services reporting to the Demo Workforce Board.

    Workspaces. A workspace is your team's container. Each person in it has one role: owner (runs the workspace and signs the report), bookkeeper (records, attaches, submits and records the funder's decision) or auditor (reads everything, changes nothing). Owners keep the lists the team shares, such as the sources of funds; owners and bookkeepers can suggest an addition. An access log shows who opened, downloaded or exported what, and when. It can be searched and exported as a CSV; it cannot be edited.

    Engagements. An engagement is one grant, contract or other agreement with a funder. Set it up from wherever you are: the proposal, the award letter or the signed contract. Every engagement walks the same eight steps: proposal, award letter, budget narrative, contract and approved budget, participants and eligibility, monthly ledger and proof, the signed report submitted by the due day, and the final audit package. Each step lists its records, and each record has a plain-words task ("Upload the award letter, or mark that none was issued"). A record the funder never issued can be marked so, with the reason, and the reason counts as the record. Clicking a saved document opens a small menu: open it, replace it with a newer file, or remove it.

    Participants. Each participant has an eligibility file, checked against a list the owners set per engagement: rename items, mark which are required, add or remove them. These are personal records (identity documents, benefit letters), so they are download only, never previewed, visible to owners and auditors, and every read is logged.

    Monthly reports. Each month, record the expenses against the approved budget lines and attach, to each one, the invoice, the evidence of payment and the allocation worksheet. For each participant paid, attach the payroll verification: timesheet, payroll register and evidence of payment. Compliance checks run as you go: some block the month until they are fixed, others ask for attention. The funder's printed form is filled from the ledger. Submit the month, then record the funder's decision (approved or denied, and the invoice number) from their email or the returned form.

    The audit package. One zip for the auditor: the letter to the auditors, the engagement records by step, each month with its ledger, its proof and its payroll verification, a participant list, the participant eligibility files (owners and auditors only), other records, a README and an index. Every download is logged.

    Overview. The first page says what is due, lists the records to check with their plain-words tasks, and shows where each engagement stands.

    Good to know
    • Documents are private files, opened through links that expire, and never sent by email. Allowed uploads: PDF, PNG, JPG, XLSX and DOCX, each recorded with its size and fingerprint.
